Alongside the [_Kojiki_](/blog/2006/06/japanese-mythology-origins/), the oldest
extant Japanese text, the _Nihonshoki_ (日本書紀), or _Nihongi_ (日本紀), is the
second most important source of _Shinwa_. It was compiled in the Nara period
(710–794), more precisely in 720 AD, under the supervision of Prince Toneri. The
_Nihonshoki_ is much richer in mythological and historical events, facts, and
stories than its predecessor, the _Kojiki_.

It is divided into 30 chapters, each dealing with an emperor, except the first
two, which contain mythical stories. Together they cover the period from the
beginning of the world to the reign of Emperor Jito in 697. Unlike the _Kojiki_,
the _Nihongi_ was compiled from many documents, including Chinese and Korean
Baekje texts. Not only does it refer to these sources throughout the book, but
it also cites different versions of some events, attributing each to its
original text.

Both the merits of virtuous rulers and the mistakes of bad ones are mentioned in
the _Nihongi_, which highlights its relative objectivity compared to the
_Kojiki_. Another substantial difference between the two is that the _Kojiki_
was written in transliterated Japanese using Chinese characters, whereas the
_Nihonshoki_ was written in classical Chinese, as was common for official
documents at the time.
