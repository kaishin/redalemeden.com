When
[_Susanowo_ defeated _Yamata no Orochi_](/blog/2006/10/shinwa-orochi-the-eight-headed-snake/),
he went looking for a place to celebrate his marriage with _Kushinada-hime_. He
ended up in
[Izumo province](https://web.archive.org/web/20070621221523/http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/0/0b/Japan_prov_map_izumo.PNG)
and said 「ここに来て、私の心はすがすがしい」 (_Koko ni kite, watashi no kokoro
wa sugasugashii_), which can be translated as "Now that I have arrived here, my
heart is refreshed." And so the place was called Suga, from the adjective he
used, すがすがしい (_sugasugashii_), to describe his state of mind.

He then built a palace in Suga, now considered Suga Shrine in Izumo. When the
clouds rose, he composed the first poem in Japan. The 31-syllable poem is called
a [_tanka_](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waka_(poetry)>). _Tanka_ are an older
form of Japanese poetry than _haiku_. They always follow the mora pattern
5-7-5-7-7:

「八雲立つ　出雲八重垣　妻ごみに　八重垣作る　その八重垣を」

(_Yakumo-tatsu_ / _Izumo Yaegaki_ / _Tsumagome-ni_ / _Yaegaki-tsukuru_ / _sono
Yaegaki-wo_)

_Susanowo_'s _tanka_ can be translated as:

> Many clouds arise.  
> The manifold fence of the forth-issuing clouds  
> Makes a manifold fence  
> For the spouses to be within.  
> Oh! that manifold fence.

After this, _Susanowo_ and _Kushinada-hime_ slept together and had a child named
_Ohona-muchi (no Kami)_. Then _Susanowo_ designated _Ashinazuchi_ and
_Tenazuchi_ as masters of the palace and bestowed upon them the title _Inada no
Miya-nushi no Kami_.
