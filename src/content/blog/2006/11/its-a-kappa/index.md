I mentioned the _Kappa_ in the [previous post](/blog/2006/11/to-err-is-human/),
and this is the perfect opportunity to introduce you to one of the most
prominent _obake_ around. _Obake_ (お化け), also called _obakemono_, _bakemono_,
or _youkai_, are ghosts, goblins, and monsters in Japanese folklore.

The _Kappa_ is an aquatic creature with a humanoid shape, a monkey face, a
predatory beak, a turtle shell, and frog limbs. Its scaly skin can have various
colors, but shades of blue and green are the most common. The amphibian creature
lives in rivers and ventures out when hungry. Its favorite meals are children,
_shirikodama_ (尻子玉, a mythical energy ball in the anus), and cucumbers.

The _Kappa_ has a water-containing organ on top of its head, surrounded by bushy
hair, that allows it to stay alive on land. It is said that if the water spills,
the creature dies instantly. This is why some people bow to the _Kappa_ so that
it bows back and unintentionally spills the water keeping it alive. _Kappa_ are
skilled in traditional medicine and can communicate perfectly in Japanese.

_Kappa_ are an intrinsic part of Japanese culture, and you may even stumble
across a sign warning kids to stay away from the river so they don't end up in a
_Kappa_'s intestines. However, none of the people who reported sighting a
_Kappa_ were able to take a picture of the creature.

It is said that _Kappa_ developed from an ancient Japanese practice of floating
stillborn fetuses down rivers and streams. Another theory is that _Kappa_ were
invented to scare children away from rivers or rice paddies, using the swollen
anus common in drowning victims as "proof" of having one's _shirikodama_ stolen,
according to Wikipedia's entry.

And yes, even _Kappa_ get swept away by the current!
