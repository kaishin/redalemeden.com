[Takeda Shingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeda_Shingen) 武田信玄 is my
favorite feudal ruler (or [_daimyo_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimyo)
大名), and this isn't the first time I've used an illustration of him on my
banner. To my delight, the new
[_taiga dorama_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiga_dorama) 大河ドラマ is about
one of Takeda's men, Yamamoto Kansuke, a lonely genius of strategy in the
16th-century [_Sengoku_ era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengoku_era). It's
NHK's annual treat: a historical TV series of 49 episodes, usually featuring
some of the most talented actors and actresses.

Kansuke works under Takeda Shingen and pursues his dream of unifying the whole
country by aiding the warlord of Kai. At the same time, he supports Shingen's
concubine Yu out of selfless affection for her. She hates but eventually comes
to love the warlord who killed her father and family. With the aid of Kansuke's
strategies, Shingen expands his power northward, where he is destined to fight
Kenshin Uesugi, the warlord of Echigo. The drama portrays how those who fight
for their dreams face their destinies in this turbulent age.

The _dorama_'s title is _Fuurin Kazan_
([風林火山](https://web.archive.org/web/20070120133341/http://www.nhk.or.jp:80/taiga/)),
written with four _kanji_ referring to wind, forest, fire and mountain. It is a
[_yojijukugo_](/blog/2006/05/kotowaza-vol-2/) meaning "Swift as wind, quiet as
forest, fierce as fire, and immovable as a mountain." It was Takeda Shingen's
motto, quoted from Sun-tzu. As a guide to victory in battle, it says that the
key is to judge the situation correctly and take appropriate action at the right
time.
