[Last time](/neosamurai/2006/05/kotowaza-vol-1/), I mentioned the three types of
_kotowaza_, based on their grammatical and lexical constructions. Today I am
going to dive into the magical realm of _yojijukugo_.

_Yojijukugo_ are commonly known in English as "four-character compounds",
"four-character idioms", or "four-character idiomatic compounds". In an
idiomatic compound, the meaning can't be directly inferred from the separate
meanings of each _kanji_ in it. That said, it is wrong to believe that every
_yojijukugo_ has an idiomatic meaning, as there are thousands of non-idiomatic
four-_kanji_ compounds. _Yojijukugo_ come from classical Chinese literature,
Buddhist literature and scriptures, old Japanese customs and proverbs, and
historical and contemporary Japanese life and social experience. (Source:
Wikipedia)

Today's treat:

- 相手次第 (あいて　しだい / _Aite shidai_)

  Being up to the other party / Determining one's attitude or response based on
  the character, attitude, or moves of the other party.

- 青田売買 (あおた　ばいばい / _Aota baibai_)

  Dealing in an unharvested rice crop / One shouldn't get ahead of events and
  make plans based on uncertain facts.

- 悪事千里 (あくじ　せんり / _Akuji senri_)

  Bad news travels fast / Ill news spreads like wildfire.

- 悪木盗泉 (あくぼく　とうせん / _Akuboku tousen_)

  A virtuous person does not commit an act that offends his moral principles, no
  matter how hard-pressed he may be.
