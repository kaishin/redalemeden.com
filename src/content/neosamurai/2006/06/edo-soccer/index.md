Today I had two stories to write about: one about art, the other about soccer.
Bringing the two together seemed almost impossible, and "almost" means there's
always a chance! Luckily, I stumbled upon a piece of art that brings it all
together:

[This poster](https://web.archive.org/web/20060702063138/http://www.picassomio.com:80/art/24884/en/)
was submitted by Tenmyouya Hisashi (天明屋尚) to an
[international art contest organized in Germany for the 2006 World Cup](https://web.archive.org/web/20060617233357/http://www.artfootball.de:80/en/artists/).
The Boys in Blue, sometimes referred to as the "Grandsons of Samurai" abroad,
were utterly defeated by the Brazilian team in their last chance to prove they
were worth more than a 1–3 defeat against the Aussies and a draw with Croatia.

As for Tenmyouya Hisashi, he is one of the "hottest" contemporary Japanese
artists. Born in Tokyo in 1966 and now living in Saitama, he is a self-taught
artist who blends different cultural elements to perfection. Every work is full
of figurative and symbolic details drawn from various modern and traditional
cultures. Tenmyouya uses traditional Japanese drawing techniques to depict a
distorted image of Japan, emphasizing the stereotypes that _gaijin_ have of the
country. His works also convey the idea of cultural fusion through his heavy use
of universal, often American, cultural symbols.

What makes his works even more mysterious is their "sarcastic disharmony".
Hisashi injects a dose of dark satire that leaves the perplexed viewer even more
confused.
