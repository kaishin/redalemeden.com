The association of soccer and _samurai_ is becoming more and more popular.
Konami has recently launched a campaign site to promote
[Winning Eleven 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winning_Eleven). The face of
their campaign is a _samurai_ with the number 10 on his back, referring both to
the tenth version of the game and to the number traditionally worn by a team's
leading attacker in soccer.

The campaign is titled ウイイレ侍がゆく！！, which means “The _Wiire Samurai_
has gone” (based on my elementary knowledge of Japanese)!

By the way, _Wiire_ ウイイレ (pronounced “wee-rei”) is the abbreviation of
Winning Eleven, pronounced in Japan as ウイニングイレベン (_Uiningu Ireben_).
Combine the first two _katakana_ of each word and you get ウイイレ: _Uiire_, or
_Wiire_!

Actually, in Japan, almost every complicated word is abbreviated!
