If there's one thing I despise, it's _cultural stereotypes_. Every culture and
country gets stereotyped elsewhere in the world, but I have a feeling that
_Nippon_ is one of the most stereotyped countries on the planet. Or maybe that's
just me!

[This article](https://web.archive.org/web/20060912080109/http://www.indiana.edu/%7Ejapan/Digests/press-pfv.htm)
talks about how _Nippon_ and _Nipponjin_ are portrayed in American mainstream
media. As it points out, Japan is often misrepresented in the American and
European press. Since the press is many people's main source of information,
they end up with a distorted image of the country.

I've met people from different parts of the world, each with their own
“selection” of stereotypes, so unfounded and nonsensical that it would be
degrading to repeat them here. One website collects examples of the kinds of
thoughts that cross people's minds about _Nippon_ (be warned, some of the
language is offensive).

Stereotypes also have a sort of scale. For some, Japan is only sushi and sumo;
for others, it's only anime and video games, the latter being a more
knowledgeable stereotype. For many _gaijin_ who have never been to Japan,
_Nipponjin_ are mysterious people, different from other humans, so the way they
think and feel must be different too…

Nowadays, with the internet, blogs, and matching services, things are getting
much better, and the image people have of _Nippon_ is starting to change. But it
doesn't seem like everyone is ready to listen to others and understand their
thoughts and feelings. Ignorance is humanity's number one enemy!
