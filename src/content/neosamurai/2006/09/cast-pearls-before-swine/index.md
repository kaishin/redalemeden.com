If you've ever wondered how to say "cast pearls before swine" in Japanese, now's
the time to learn one of my favorite _kotowaza_. If you already know it, feel
free to skip what follows!

In Japanese, it's 猫に小判,
[_neko ni koban_](https://web.archive.org/web/20060511230143/http://www.geocities.com:80/Tokyo/5623/06.wav)
(click the link to listen), literally "(Give) coins to a cat". It means don't
offer something to someone who is incapable of using it properly. Obviously, a
cat won't understand what coins are for, at least under normal circumstances.
Who knows…

A _koban_ is an oval Japanese gold coin that was used in the Edo period. And
since [Pokémon Diamond and Pearl](https://www.pokemon.co.jp/game/ds/dp/) were
released two days ago in Japan (yes, yes, I'm an _otaku_ when it comes to
Pokémon games), I couldn't come up with a better image than the famous
mischievous cat Pokémon, [Meowth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meowth), or
_Nyasu_ in the original version. The coin on his forehead is actually a _koban_.

There's a closer equivalent to "cast pearls before swine": 豚に真珠, _buta ni
shinju_, "(Give) a pearl to a pig". But since I like cats more than pigs, I'd
rather use the first!

And one last thing: since _koban_ aren't used anymore, maybe people will start
saying 猫に札, _neko ni satsu_, "(Give) bills to a cat", like a 5,000-yen bill.
