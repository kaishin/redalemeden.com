I had a brief chat with Tokyo Dan, the man behind
[What's Happening in Tokyo](https://web.archive.org/web/20061004203330/http://www.whitokyo.info:80/WHIT.htm),
or WHIT, a great podcast featuring indie bands and artists from Japan that Dan
takes the trouble, or pleasure, to track down himself! I've been listening since
the podcast's early days, and it's getting more and more elaborate.

Tokyo Dan coined the term Jindies to refer to Japanese independent music. I
can't verify that claim, but there's no doubt that he spread the word about it.
In Japanese, the concept of _doujin_ is used to refer to independent art and
leisure activities. _Doujin_ means "same person" and refers to people who share
the same hobbies. _Doujinshi_ refers to magazines or comics published by fans
for fans, and _doujin ongaku_ conveys the same idea when talking about music.

Jindies are flourishing more than ever now, thanks to the internet, and more
specifically, web 2.0 technologies. Blogs, podcasts and online shops have made
things a whole lot easier for aspiring artists who don't want to sign contracts
with anyone.

I discovered rock Jindies way before I started listening to WHIT, when I
realized that most bands perform way better when they're independent than after
they sign with a major record label. WHIT came along afterwards to confirm that
there are indeed some great artists underground who hardly reach the ears of
those enslaved by Sony Music and Toshiba-EMI.

But what really got me into _doujin ongaku_, and prompted me to go "off-topic"
on Neo Samurai, are those websites brimming with talented artists who offer
their tunes for free to people who love music! Websites such as the Japanese
[MF247](https://web.archive.org/web/20061104173709/http://www.mf247.jp:80/view/index.php/)
and the international
[Podsafe Music Network](https://web.archive.org/web/20061108192922/http://music.podshow.com:80/),
to name a couple, are becoming more and more popular. They let users rate,
comment on, recommend, share and, most importantly, enjoy the free tunes.
Artists and listeners aren't the only winners: these sites also let podcasters
get past the hassles of copyright and embellish their shows with various music
genres.

It seems like I got carried away a little bit, but I really do appreciate
_doujin ongaku_, or Jindies, and I'd like anyone who really loves Japanese music
to support those artists in one way or another! Look forward to my next
"off-topic"!
