メリークリスマス!

This is what people say in Japan this season. "Huh? Are the Japanese
Christians?" those of you who know even less about Japanese culture than I do
may wonder. Well, the answer is no, it's just Western influence. Actually, Japan
isn't alone. China, Korea, North Africa and other corners of the planet
celebrate Xmas in their own way, even without a connection to Christianity.
Blame or thank globalization!

Anyway, I'm spending this season in front of my computer, in a chilly square
room, browsing the web for interesting stories about how Xmas is celebrated in
the land of the rising sun!

Talking about the rising sun, the _Hinomaru_ goes quite well with Santa's
colors. A [SantaCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santarchy) was held in Kyoto,
according to 3yen News, alongside crowds celebrating Emperor Akihito's 73rd
birthday. Others prefer to go solo in Santa garb to catch the attention of
photographers wandering Harajuku, as Masa reported on his website (beware, it
contains uncensored, questionable material).

As you might or might not know, 2007 is the year of the boar (_inoshishi_). It's
no coincidence that people flocked to an amusement park in Oita prefecture to
photograph an albino wild boar called "Bu-chan": they want to use photographs of
the animal on New Year cards.

In Aichi prefecture, a shortage of snow has prompted ski resort managers to use
artificial snow to give visitors a white Christmas.

It's pretty hard to miss the typo in the title of this entry. But let me tell
you, it isn't a typo. It's a reference to one of the hottest items this
Christmas in Japan and elsewhere: the Nintendo Wii. The console has caused quite
a stir worldwide, thanks to its wand-like remote controller, attractive design
and extended game library (the Virtual Console lets gamers download old hits in
exchange for Wii Points). Too bad I have to wait a little longer to get my hands
on a Japanese Wii and start [vlogging about it](https://surrealu.blogspot.com/)!
