Another year comes and goes, time keeps flowing, and so does the countdown of
our lives…

A lot happened in these 365 days, though. Of course, I am talking about the
country in the far east of the Asian continent, commonly called Japan, _Nippon_,
or the land of the rising sun.

If you've been hibernating since January 2006, then it's time to catch up:

To Japan's delight, the imperial family finally welcomed a male heir, Prince
Hisahito, son of Prince Akishino and Princess Kiko. Discussions about revising
the law to allow female heirs to succeed to the throne were all halted.

The Elvis impersonator, Junichirou Koizumi, finally stepped down, leaving his
post as prime minister to Shinzou Abe, who won a landslide victory against his
rivals. Before this, the Yasukuni Shrine issue caused a diplomatic uproar and
further widened the gap between Japan and its closest neighbors, South Korea and
China, adding to other issues such as territorial disputes and historical
controversies. Abe made official visits to Beijing and Seoul in an attempt to
mend diplomatic relations.

North Korea and its nuclear program are also emerging as a serious threat to
Japan's security. North Korea categorically refused to accept Japan in the
six-party talks after Japan imposed economic and diplomatic sanctions in
response to its underground nuclear tests.

Abe's educational reforms and plans to upgrade the Defense Agency didn't stop
his popularity from falling after a series of scandals involving his office.
Rumors of his imminent resignation have recently started to spread.

Sports-wise, Japan has reaped several international victories in 2006, thanks in
particular to the ice queen, Shizuka Arakawa, who won Japan's only gold medal at
the Turin Winter Olympics. Team Japan also proved to the world that Japan has
some of the world's best baseball players by coming first in the inaugural World
Baseball Classic. Recent transfers of Japanese players, both at home and across
the Pacific, further proved this.

Besides the slight economic recovery, corporate scandals were the biggest
stories in business news in 2006. First came the Livedoor scandal involving
Takafumi Horie, or "Horiemon", one of the most prominent Japanese businessmen of
the 21st century, who was nabbed for securities fraud. Then came Yoshiaki
Murakami of M&A Consulting Inc., who was arrested for insider trading.

On the social front, bullying, suicides and drunk driving were the big stories
of 2006. The government is taking serious punitive measures against drunk
drivers, those who accompany them and even those who provide the booze.
Meanwhile, schools are struggling to cope with the rising number of bullied
pupils taking their own lives. Some schools tried emergency measures, but
without effective government intervention to tackle the roots of the problem,
the results are barely noticeable.

2006 was undeniably the year of high-end technology, from humanoid androids and
patrol robots to the new generation of home consoles, Nintendo's Wii and Sony's
PS3, both launched this year. Along the way came various new technologies that
will be part of daily life in the near future.

Happy New Year, Japan!
