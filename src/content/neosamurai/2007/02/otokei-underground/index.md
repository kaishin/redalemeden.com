As you might or might not know, I am a big fan of the Japanese independent music
scene. About 75% of all the music I listen to is by amateur and independent
Japanese artists. In Japanese, these artists are called _otokei_ 音系, and their
music is referred to as _otokei doujin_ or _doujin ongaku_ 同人音楽. They
produce and publish their music themselves, without any major label backing them
up.

In Japanese, _doujin_ is used to refer to any independently published work, be
it manga, music, or literature. _Doujin_ means "same person" and refers to
people who share the "same" hobbies. _Doujinshi_ refers to magazines or comics
published by fans for fans, and _otokei doujin_ conveys the same idea in music.
Tokyo Dan, the man behind the
"[What's Happening in Tokyo](https://web.archive.org/web/20070227165700/http://www.whitokyo.info:80/WHIT.htm)"
podcast, coined the term "Jindies" as an English alternative. For further
details, check Wikipedia in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dojin_music)
or
[Japanese](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9F%B3%E7%B3%BB%E5%90%8C%E4%BA%BA).

I do listen to virtually all musical genres, but I have a penchant for metal,
rock, instrumentals, and game music. MintJam, co-founded by a2c, is my favorite
indie band ever.

MintJam was formed by Setzer and a2c in April 2003. They started as an amateur
band sharing their music on the Japanese indie community
[Muzie](https://web.archive.org/web/20070514134052/http://www.muzie.co.jp/).
They refined their style and performance when kya joined later that year,
becoming a widely recognized indie band. Their activities expanded when TERRA
and A. Koga joined in December 2004, although Koga left shortly after. In 2006,
kya also left the band.

Their highly technical playing style and elaborate melodies are MintJam's
selling points. Even though their arrangements are faithful to the original
works, they often feature lengthy melodic guitar solos and more elaborate
rhythms. Two of their most acclaimed original songs are "Rival" and "A Rude
Performance", while their covers of "Theme of Arcueid" and "Romancing SaGa 3:
Last Battle" are perhaps their most famous game music arrangements.
